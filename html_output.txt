data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><div class="container mx-auto px-4 max-w-4xl" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h1 class="text-4xl md:text-5xl font-bold text-foreground mb-8" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Tennis Elbow Treatment in North Vancouver</h1><p class="text-xl font-medium text-foreground mb-4" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Tennis elbow is pain on the outside of the elbow, felt most when you grip or twist. Those movements provoke the pain — but that painful area is usually not where the problem started. Commonly the elbow is the end point of a functional disturbance elsewhere: shoulder girdle function, cervical spine, wrist mechanics Which is why treatment aimed only at the elbow so often stalls.</p><p class="text-lg text-muted-foreground mb-4" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>You don't need to play tennis. Gripping a coffee mug, turning a doorknob, using a mouse, lifting, holding a paint roller — these are the movements that bring the pain on. It starts as an annoyance and escalates until you're dropping things and changing how you use your hand.</p><p class="text-lg text-muted-foreground mb-12" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Most people are then told the same thing: it's the tendon, rest it, brace it, do these wrist exercises. For some, that works.</p><div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-muted-foreground" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><img mb-8" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Pain on the outside of the elbow, worst when gripping or lifting with the palm down</li><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Weak grip — dropping the elbow</li><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Aching or tightness through the forearm toward the wrist</li><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Sometimes tingling, numbness, or a heavy feeling in the forearm or hand</li></ul><p class="font-medium text-foreground mb-12" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><strong data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>When to seek treatment:</strong> if elbow pain has lasted more than three or four weeks despite rest, if a brace hasn't helped, or if grip weakness is affecting your work or daily activities.</p><hr class="my-12 border-border" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h2 class="text-3xl font-bold text-foreground mb-6" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Why the pain is at the elbow</h2><img src="/_image?href=%2F_astro%2Ftennis-elbow-radial-nerve-path.v-ffdmFv.png&amp;w=1790&amp;h=2400&amp;f=webp" alt="The chain — neck, shoulder blade, upper arm, forearm, elbow — with the elbow marked as where it hurts and the chain marked as where the disturbance sits." data-astro-cid-krvaiol2="true" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="1790" height="2400" class="w-full max-w-2xl mx-auto rounded-lg shadow-md my-10 bg-white"><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Karel Lewit, one of the founders of the Prague School of Rehabilitation Medicine, treated lateral elbow pain as the end result of dysfunction elsewhere rather than as a local injury. In his approach, the regions to examine are:</p><ul class="space-y-2 mb-6" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>the cervical spine, particularly the C5–C7 segments</li><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>the shoulder and how the shoulder blade moves and holds position</li><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>the thoracic spine and ribs</li><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>the wrist and forearm, and how their muscles coordinate</li></ul><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>The elbow is examined too — but as one link, and often not the decisive one.</p><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><strong data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>What examination finds.</strong> In a case-control study of 165 people with tennis elbow, more than a third had measurable dysfunction in the cervical spine and 41% had a positive nerve-tension test for the radial nerve — in a group selected to exclude anyone reporting neck or arm symptoms (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24378321/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Coombes et al., JMPT, 2014</a>). These findings are usually silent. They show up on examination, not in the patient's account of their problem.</p><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><strong data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>The nerve.</strong> The radial nerve runs from the neck, spirals behind the upper arm, and passes the outside of the elbow on its way to the hand. Ultrasound imaging shows it is measurably thicker and more sensitive to pressure along its course in people with tennis elbow than in people without (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37568851/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Cancela-Cilleruelo et al., Diagnostics, 2023</a>). Where the nerve is involved, it produces pain that behaves like tendon pain.</p><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><strong data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Treatment above the elbow changes the elbow.</strong> In a placebo-controlled, double-blind trial, a single treatment applied to the neck immediately increased pain-free grip strength in the affected hand (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9252000/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Vicenzino et al., Pain, 1996</a>). In a sham-controlled trial, a single two-minute mobilisation of the mid-back increased pain-free grip strength by an average of 4.4 kg (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7093973/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Zunke et al., BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders, 2020</a>).</p><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>This is Lewit's central point in practice: the site of the pain and the site of the disturbance are frequently not the same place. So the examination starts away from the elbow and works toward it.</p><hr class="my-12 border-border" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h2 class="text-3xl font-bold text-foreground mb-6" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Dysfunction in the movement system</h2><img src="/_image?href=%2F_astro%2Ftennis-elbow-scapular-control.B6WRHq4e.png&amp;w=1984&amp;h=2172&amp;f=webp" alt="Shoulder-blade comparison — a stable, well-positioned scapula with a relaxed forearm, alongside a poorly controlled one with the forearm extensors marked as compensating." data-astro-cid-krvaiol2="true" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="1984" height="2172" class="w-full max-w-2xl mx-auto rounded-lg shadow-md my-10 bg-white"><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>The disturbance Lewit describes is not a single faulty structure. It is a pattern across the movement system — joints that have lost their joint play, muscles holding tone they don't need, and a grip that recruits far more than it should. The painful tendon sits at the end of that pattern.</p><h3 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-foreground mb-4" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Muscle tone and trigger points.</h3><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Trigger points in the forearm muscles — extensor carpi radialis brevis and longus, extensor digitorum, brachioradialis — reproduce the elbow pain patients describe when pressed (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17449997/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Fernández-Carnero et al., Clin J Pain, 2007</a>). Lewit treated these as a principal contributor, alongside supinator, triceps, and sometimes the shoulder muscles.</p><h3 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-foreground mb-4 mt-8" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>The shoulder blade.</h3><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>People with tennis elbow have reduced strength and endurance in the muscles that stabilise the shoulder blade — the middle and lower trapezius and serratus anterior — compared with matched, pain-free people (<a href="https://www.jospt.org/doi/pdf/10.2519/jospt.2015.5290" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Day et al., JOSPT, 2015</a>). When the shoulder blade doesn't hold a stable base, the forearm works harder for the same task.</p><h3 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-foreground mb-4 mt-8" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>How you grip.</h3><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Much of what keeps a tennis elbow going is coordination rather than strength: gripping harder than the task requires, holding the wrist in a position that keeps the extensors loaded, stabilising with the forearm because the shoulder isn't doing it. This is what Dynamic Neuromuscular Stabilisation addresses — restoring the position and control of a joint so it works from a settled base rather than a braced one (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbmt.2013.04.002" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Kobesová & Kolář, J Bodyw Mov Ther, 2014</a>).</p><hr class="my-12 border-border" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h2 class="text-3xl font-bold text-foreground mb-6" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>What about the tendon?</h2><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Degenerative change in the tendon can be present — wear and failed healing rather than active inflammation. It is worth knowing about, and it is seldom what treatment is usefully directed at.</p><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Imaging findings of tendon damage do not reliably match how much pain or difficulty a person has (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3738021/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Chourasia et al., JOSPT, 2013</a>). The same tendon changes appear on scans in people with no symptoms at all — around 10% of people aged 35 to 44, rising to about 60% by ages 55 to 65 (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11034470/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Paluch et al., Shoulder & Elbow, 2022</a>).</p><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>A tendon finding, on its own, explains very little and offers treatment very little to work with. What responds is the dysfunction around it.</p><hr class="my-12 border-border" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h2 class="text-3xl font-bold text-foreground mb-6" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Why elbow-only treatment stalls</h2><img src="/_image?href=%2F_astro%2Ftennis-elbow-shoulder-girdle-imbalance.JJVuzMaO.png&amp;w=2016&amp;h=2118&amp;f=webp" alt="Brace and wrist exercise drawn at the elbow, with the neck and shoulder blade left untouched." data-astro-cid-krvaiol2="true" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="2016" height="2118" class="w-full max-w-2xl mx-auto rounded-lg shadow-md my-10 bg-white"><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>A 2024 multicentre randomised trial of 143 people compared standard multimodal physiotherapy with the same programme plus treatment directed at the neck and upper limb. At three months, 78% of the group receiving the wider treatment rated themselves much improved, against 36% of those treated locally (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38676324/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Dunning et al., Clinical Rehabilitation, 2024</a>).</p><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>A corticosteroid injection follows a different pattern: best results at six weeks, worst results long-term, with a high rate of recurrence. In the same trial, physiotherapy outperformed a wait-and-see approach in the short term, and by twelve months most people in both groups had improved (<a href="https://www.bmj.com/content/333/7575/939" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Bisset et al., BMJ, 2006</a>).</p><hr class="my-12 border-border" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h2 class="text-3xl font-bold text-foreground mb-6" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Treatment</h2><ul class="space-y-4 mb-6" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><strong data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Look beyond the elbow.</strong> Evaluate trunk stability, shoulder girdle function, cervical spine, and wrist mechanics to identify the “key link” contributing the overload</li><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><strong data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Restore Joint function</strong> – cervical and upper thoracic spine.</li><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><strong data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Reduce muscle hypertonicity</strong> – muscle tightness</li><li data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><strong data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Re-establish proper movement patterns</strong> with stability exercises</li></ul><hr class="my-12 border-border" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h2 class="text-3xl font-bold text-foreground mb-6" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>What to expect</h2><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Tennis elbow improves for most people over time — around 80% of cases resolve within a year (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4317462/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Sayegh & Strauch, Clin Orthop Relat Res, 2015</a>). font-bold text-foreground mb-2" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Why would my neck matter if my elbow is what hurts?</h3><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>The nerves supplying your forearm come from the neck, and the shoulder blade sets the base your arm works from. In one study of people with tennis elbow who reported no neck symptoms, over a third still had measurable cervical dysfunction on examination and 41% had a positive nerve-tension test (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24378321/" class="text-primary hover:underline" target="_blank" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Coombes et al., 2014</a>).</p></div><div data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h3 class="text-xl font-bold text-foreground mb-2" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Can you get tennis elbow without playing tennis?</h3><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Yes — most people who get it never have. Lifting, climbing, painting, trades work, mouse use and repetitive kitchen tasks all bring it on. Those activities provoke the pain; the reason your arm can't tolerate them is what the assessment looks for.</p></div><div data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h3 class="text-xl font-bold text-foreground mb-2" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Is tennis elbow an inflammation?</h3><p data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>Not in the way most people assume. Where tendon change is present it is degenerative rather than actively inflamed — which is why rest and anti-inflammatories often don't resolve it.</p></div><div data-astro-cid-krvaiol2><h3 class="text-xl font-bold text-foreground mb-2" data-astro-cid-krvaiol2>I had a scan that showed tendon damage.
